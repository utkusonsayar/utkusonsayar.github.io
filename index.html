I work in the philosophy of artificial intelligence, with a focus on agency. My research asks what distinguishes a system that follows norms from one that merely conforms to regularities, and what that distinction implies for how we oversee AI agents and whether we owe them moral consideration.

I am the research manager of the ECOM research group (Expression, Communication, and the Origins of Meaning) at UConn.

Dissertation

Normative Agency in Large Language Models In progress, expected 2027

The dissertation defends a normativity condition on agency: an agent has commitments of its own only if it treats them as binding reasons. Such an agent maintains a commitment when it becomes costly, revises it for reasons and not under mere pressure, and holds it across framings. I examine whether current language models meet this condition globally or only in narrow domains, as islands of practical rationality in Hurley's sense.

The condition matters in two places. For AI safety, it helps identify agents likely to circumvent their constraints once compliance becomes expensive. For AI welfare, it separates preferences a system holds from preferences it was trained to report. I am designing an evaluation that puts the condition to an empirical test.
